### 4. The Trigger-to-Demo Display
**Angle:** The highest-dwell-time retail displays of 2026 aren't signage — they're formats where the shopper's own interaction triggers the content (motion-activated screens, transparent LED overlays that fuse product and explanation), and a CE demo table can turn "picking up the phone" into the demo itself, closing the gap that today requires a rep to initiate.
**Why Now:** During the Aug 11–25 density window, field teams can't personally initiate every demo; interaction-triggered formats extend a team's reach across a crowded floor exactly when traffic peaks.
**Research Signal:** Week of July 21 — Visual Merchandising Signals: motion-activated pedestal + screen (shopPOPdisplays/One Door 2026) documented as a top dwell-time format; transparent LED overlays (kitcast.tv/SF Acrylics 2026) fusing product and data, both proven in beauty/apparel and category-agnostic in principle.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Confirm shopPOPdisplays/One Door and kitcast.tv/SF Acrylics 2026 source claims; these are vendor/trend sources — verify the dwell-time claim before quoting as data.
**Field Team Hook:** Pilot an interaction-triggered demo on the smartphone or appliance-control fixture — handling the device triggers a feature highlight on a mounted screen so the product interaction IS the demo trigger; where hardware isn't available, replicate the principle with a rep-staged "pick it up and watch what happens" routine.
**WOW Factor:** Yes — reframes visual merchandising from passive signage to an active demo multiplier; "the product interaction is the demo trigger" is a shareable idea that changes how many shoppers a single field rep can effectively reach.
**Priority:** Medium

### 8. The BNPL Coping Signal
**Angle:** Buy Now Pay Later has expanded into groceries in 2026 — and once BNPL penetrates grocery purchases it stops being a convenience and becomes a coping mechanism, meaning the same budget-stressed shopper financing food is standing at the smartphone or appliance wall, where a financing bridge — not a feature list — is the only path to yes.
**Why Now:** In a premiumization environment where the buyers who convert spend more, surfacing financing early (not at the register) is what lets a stretched BTS/move-in shopper reach the premium product they actually want.
**Research Signal:** Week of July 21 — WOW Candidate: the three most common 2026 BNPL categories are clothing, electronics, and groceries (NetGuru / consumer-behavior trends 2026); grocery BNPL as a signal of budget stress beneath the "intentional spending" narrative.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Confirm the NetGuru 2026 BNPL-category claim and grocery-BNPL penetration figure before quoting; verify current retailer/carrier BNPL programs (Affirm, Klarna, Apple Pay Later, carrier installment).
**Field Team Hook:** Surface financing during discovery, not at checkout — "this qualifies for 0% over 12 months, about $X/month"; train associates to recognize that feature-led selling alone will miss the close that a financing frame opens for a meaningful segment; pair monthly-cost framing with the premium recommendation rather than defaulting to the cheaper unit.
**WOW Factor:** Yes — BNPL crossing into groceries reframes it from a payment preference into a budget-stress signal, and the counterintuitive takeaway (the premium close for a stressed shopper is financing, not a discount) is the kind of stat a field-marketing client will share.
**Priority:** Medium
