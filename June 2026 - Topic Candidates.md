## Candidate Topics

### 1. "Done for the Game, Wrapped for Dad" — The 10-Day Compounded Urgency Window
**Angle:** FIFA World Cup (June 11) and Father's Day (June 21) are not two separate retail events this year — they are one compounded urgency window peaking June 7–17, and most brand activation calendars did not build for this overlap.
**Why Now:** The 10-day window closes June 17; field teams need a dual-urgency script and TV/soundbar demo setup in place by June 7, which means briefing this week. Treating these as sequential events forfeits the overlap multiplier entirely.
**Research Signal:** Week of May 18 WOW Candidate — FIFA/Father's Day convergence flagged as the most immediately actionable calendar insight of the month; June 7–17 identified as critical activation window; confirmed that consumers buying a TV for World Cup viewing often frame it as a Father's Day gift purchase.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Large-format TV YoY unit sales for June — confirm June is a TV category peak (likely Circana or NPD); World Cup TV uplift from prior FIFA cycles (2022 Qatar data may be the most recent comparable).
**Field Team Hook:** Train associates on the single verbal anchor — "This TV is available now, ready to gift by the 21st, and it'll be set up before kickoff on the 11th" — then run one dual-purpose demo that serves both the game-day and gift-giving framing simultaneously.
**WOW Factor:** Yes — a consumer shopping with both urgencies active simultaneously (gift + event) is the most highly convertible shopper profile of the month, and the overlap is structurally invisible to teams following a standard sequential Father's Day playbook.
**Priority:** High

### 2. The Reddit Anti-Feature Effect — When the Objection Was Built at Home, Not in the Aisle
**Angle:** Reddit appliance communities are actively counter-programming manufacturer feature marketing — threads warning against smart features (WiFi, touchscreens, cameras in washers/dryers/refrigerators) are indexed in Google and visible before a shopper ever reaches brand content, which means a consumer standing in front of a Whirlpool connected appliance display may have already read three threads explaining why simple is more reliable.
**Why Now:** NIQ confirmed in May 2026 that home appliance purchase drivers are shifting to quality, convenience, and hygiene — not smart features. Reddit anti-smart sentiment from two separate weeks of research (May 4 and May 11) aligns with NIQ's industry data. The objection is active now and accelerating.
**Research Signal:** Week of May 4 WOW Candidate — Reddit technicians actively counseling against WiFi, touchscreens, cameras citing higher failure rates; Week of May 11 NIQ corroboration — 2026 appliance growth driven by practical utility, not smart-feature innovation.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Whirlpool/Maytag reliability ratings vs. Samsung in current Consumer Reports or J.D. Power data (2025–2026); specific Reddit thread volume/engagement metrics on "smart appliance reliability" searches would strengthen but are hard to source — flag as observational.
**Field Team Hook:** For Whirlpool/Maytag teams: acknowledge the Reddit-sourced objection proactively before the shopper raises it — "A lot of people are reading that smart features add failure points. Here's what that actually looks like in Maytag's service data versus what Reddit is seeing in other brands." Turns a hidden objection into a trust-building moment.
**WOW Factor:** Yes — the objection field teams need to overcome is being built at home via Google-indexed Reddit threads they don't know exist, weeks before the floor visit happens.
**Priority:** High

### 4. The Pre-Prime Day Validation Visit — Converting "Just Comparing" Into Same-Day Sales

**Research Signal:** Week of May 4 — pre-Prime Day research window identified; June in-store visits framed as "high-intent but pre-purchase validation trips." Week of May 11 and May 18 — confirmed as recurring seasonal marker across all three weeks of the log.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Amazon Prime Day 2026 dates not yet announced — verify late June per typical ~3-week prior announcement pattern. Prime Day lift data by category (mobile, appliances, smart home) from Circana or NIQ for prior years.

### 5. The Section 122 Expiry Trap — What Happens When the Urgency Argument Expires
**Angle:** The 150-day Section 122 tariff clock creates a hard policy expiration window in summer–fall 2026; if Congress does not vote to renew, retailers who raised prices citing tariff cost face a credibility problem when tariffs expire but prices don't fall — and field teams carrying "buy now before prices rise further" urgency scripts need a backup rationale ready before the expiry date arrives.
**Why Now:** Section 122 tariffs at 15% took effect following the February 20 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling; 150 days from that ruling puts the clock expiry approximately in mid-July 2026 — squarely within the June issue's action horizon. Field teams that have built tariff urgency into their June scripts need to know this timeline.
**Research Signal:** Week of May 18 second-order observations — Section 122 150-day clock noted as a field-team risk; urgency messaging built purely on "prices will stay high" flagged as needing a backup rationale if the tariff situation shifts.
**Data to Verify:** ⚠️ Exact Section 122 tariff effective date — confirm the precise start date to calculate the 150-day expiry window. NRF tariff advocacy page cited; also verify: has any Congressional vote to renew been introduced or scheduled?
**Field Team Hook:** Shift urgency framing from tariff-specific ("prices are up because of tariffs") to replacement-cycle specific ("your 2020–2021 appliance is at the 5-year mark — this is the right moment regardless of tariff outcomes") — durable urgency that doesn't depend on policy staying intact.
**WOW Factor:** No
**Priority:** Medium
