## Slide 4

Store Food Compete with Larger Formats. C-Store’s quality and speed rival that of Grocery and fast food - matching Grocery, Mass, and Club on store-made food quality, and winning for fast, frictionless trips.
Impulse categories dominate. Food and beverages drive both total sales and impulse purchases, with top grab-and-go including candy, salty snacks, and baked goods.
Healthier options are in demand. Shoppers want better-for-you choices, with half seeking healthier options - most often natural/organic and clean label products.
Hot foods drive mealtime visits. Planned trips cater to real mealtimes, while impulse trips are for snacks between meals and late at night. Over half say C-store hot foods rival fast food quality.
Wellness wins. Shoppers are seeking freshness and healthier choices. As the presence of more wellness-focused foods has become a shopper request, expanding            fresh and nutritious offerings is essential.
Engagement is high and purposeful. Frequent trips reflect strong channel loyalty with 2-in-3 going at least once a week. Trips are intentional, driven by food and fuel.
Trip frequency is rising. Convenience and assortment is fueling growth with one-fourth of shoppers increasing trips, motivated by quick meals, snacks, drinks, and easy access.
Deals drive awareness and desire. Signage is key, but shoppers want more value. Most discover deals via signage in-store and at pumps. There is a desire for more BOGOs, and combo deals that allow for meal customization.
Fuel drives foot traffic and store sales. Gas trips convert into retail purchases. As 80% of trips include fuel, nearly all fuel buyers enter the store most often for tobacco, salty snacks, and beverages.
“Convenience” is Convenience’s core strength. Speed and access set C-stores apart. Shoppers value quick trips and strategic locations and consider the channel ideal for last-minute needs and fast food.

## Slide 5

Next-Level Convenience: Fast, Fresh, and Forward
Capitalize on high engagement and loyalty. Reward frequent visits, especially for fuel and food combos. Retailers should use shopper messaging to tailor messaging and promotions based on trip purpose (food vs. fuel).
Leverage rising trip frequency. With assortment and variety being a top driver of channel usage, retailers can expand quick meal offerings with RTE options for all dayparts. Retailers may want to optimize store layouts for grab-and-go by highlighting snacks, drinks, and meal kits near the front/checkout.
Make promos pop, especially BOGOs. Elevate visibility with bold, clear signage both in-store and at the pump. As BOGOs are the favored promo, ensure these deals are front and center in shopper messaging when active. Retailers may also want to trial customizable BOGO bundles to drive excitement and cross-category sales.
Fuel as a gateway to retail. Bundle fuel rewards with market purchases to increase conversion. Categories typically bought on fuel trips (tobacco, salty snacks, self-serve beverages) may also receive special advertising at the pump.
Compete on food quality. Promote store-made food as a fast, high-quality alternative to fast food. Retailers may want to consider sampling events to build trust in food quality and freshness and gauge interest on potential new food/drinks.
Maximize impulse sales. Create impulse zones with top-performing items like candy, baked goods, and salty snacks. Placement of seasonal or limited-time items can also be rotated to keep the experience “new” and exciting.
Meet the demand for healthier options. Expand on better-for-you with natural, organic, and clean-label products. Highlight health-forward items with shelf tags, signage, and digital alerts for retailer app users.
