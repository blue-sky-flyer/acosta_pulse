## Slide 3

SWEET CHOICES: The Shopper’s Journey for CANDY
Candy shoppers purchase across many different channels, and while Grocery and Mass are the top two common store types, more shoppers buy candy at dollar, drug, and convenience stores than snacks. Although physical stores are preferred purchase locations, 1-in-4 regularly buy online. Candy is typically enjoyed as a treat by the entire household.  Experience and brand trust are essential for new item trial, though often the appealing look of candy leads to impulse buying.
Brick-and-Mortar Engagement: Provide comprehensive category information and new item displays in stores to support the research and discovery phase of the purchase journey. In-store sampling may also encourage immediate consumption opportunities.
Family-Oriented Marketing: Highlight the enjoyment of candy by all household members in shopper marketing, and position candy as a versatile treat suitable for rewards or between-meal treats.
Optimize Online Presence: Utilize automated recommendations to suggest products based on preferences and purchase history.
Innovation in Flavor and Packaging: Introduce new flavors, including limited editions and attractive packaging, that can satisfy a craving or generate impulse trial.
Build Brand Trust: With trust being a key choice driver, retailers can highlight familiar and trusted brands to encourage new item trial and maintain category health.

## Slide 4

CRUNCH TIME: The Shopper’s Journey for SNACKS
Snack shoppers favor both traditional and online channels, purchasing snacks a few times a week to once a month. Consumed by the whole household, snacks are most often used as between-meal snacks. While typically a planned routine purchase, impulse buys are triggered by visual appeal and cravings. One-in-four purchase snacks online, with retailer sites and automated recommendations being top point-of-sale information sources.
Leverage Brick-and-Mortar: Maintain a strong presence particularly at Grocery and Mass, as most shoppers turn to these first to buy, to seek information, and especially to find new snack items.
Impulse Purchase Strategies: Retailers should enhance both, a) the visual appeal of snack displays to attract impulse buys on trips, and b) the attractive deals to trigger cravings and spontaneous purchases.
Digital Presence: Retailer websites – the shopper’s first online stop to search for snacks – should be user-friendly with automated suggestions for a curated snacks list. Just as in stores, visual appeal and deals are top online triggers, particularly for impulse buys.
The Cost of Health: With the main reasons for lapsed buying being ‘healthiness’ and ‘price,’ brands and stores should prioritize highlighting natural, nutrition, and ingredient-based health benefits, as well as deals that make them more affordable.
Wide Variety: Maintain a diverse range of snacks to keep consumer interest high. This shopper is very open to new items, but
ensure trusted mainstay brands are retained, as shoppers use them for a comparative when considering new snacks.
