Shoppers shared their store choice priorities using a trade-off exercise
MaxDiff helps us uncover true preferences by making respondents choose both the best and worst options from a set of attributes which helps us understand what matters most to shoppers when choosing where to shop!
What is MaxDiff?
MaxDiff, is short for maximum difference scaling, it’s a survey-based research technique used to quantify preferences.
It goes beyond standard rating questions by forcing respondents to choose both the most important and least important items from a list.
MaxDiff is sometimes referred to as “best-worst scaling.”
The outcome of the modeling are preference scores that represent the utility respondents perceive with each attribute.

Q24 (1/4) From the choices below, please choose which area of the store is the most and least important to you when you are choosing where you'll shop most often

Process:
The MaxDiff survey exercise randomly presents 4 of 30 store areas and asks respondents which one is the most important to them when they are choosing a store they will shop most often.  And which area is the least important.
As respondents trade off on the randomly selected areas the preference shares are calculated to determine the probability that that store areas would be the most influential or important in their choice of which store they shop most often.

Process:
The MaxDiff survey exercise randomly presents 4 of 30 store areas and asks respondents which one is the most important to them when they are choosing a store they will shop most often.  And which area is the least important.
As respondents trade off on the randomly selected areas the preference shares are calculated to determine the probability that that store areas would be the most influential or important in their choice of which store they shop most often.
A series of 4 question sets are presented to each respondent.

Priority order based on MaxDiff the calculated Preference Share – Preference share = the likelihood that the area is truly the most preferred.

**How does this compare to the feature counts or the Probability The Area Would Be Selected As “Most Important”?
The feature counts presented in a bar graph on a prior slide simply represent the % of time the area was selected as the most important when it was presented.  The preference shares are actual probability calculations.  So the order and magnitude of difference will differ slightly.

Weighted Probabilities: This method incorporates the principles of the multinomial logit modeling used to derive the MaxDiff utility preferences. It calculates the probability that a combination of items would be selected as most preferred, weighted by the item’s preference share.
