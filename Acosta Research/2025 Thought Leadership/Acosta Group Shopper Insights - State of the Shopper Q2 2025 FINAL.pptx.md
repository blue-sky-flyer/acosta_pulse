## Slide 3

Summary -- Shoppers stay cautious amid economic uncertainty and tariff tensions
Shoppers negatively describe the current economy in line with University of Michigan sentiment data which has dropped 30% in 2025 so far due to growing worries about a trade war
However, relatively few shoppers have a poor outlook of their household’s financial situation and job security
Those pessimistic about their finances are more likely vs. those optimistic to already be cutting back on “extras” due to challenging times
Shoppers are still value seeking in CPG, but their behavior in cutting back on discretionary spending such as eating out, travel, and entertainment has moderated in line with lower inflation (for now)
Shoppers are dealing with financial uncertainty by shopping value channels more frequently and continuing to be diligent on savings-oriented behaviors (e.g., buying cheaper brands, stocking up, buying/wasting less, etc.) when grocery shopping
While consumers are still impacted by higher prices on food away from home, eating out is the #1 area consumers intend to treat themselves when they splurge
While shoppers enjoy buying store brands, the number of those who say they are buying more store brands to deal with higher prices has leveled off in grocery aligned with slowing store brands’ market share growth
Shoppers say that name brands stand out for quality, design, and taste, while store brands primarily stand out for value, but for many features, there is no difference
Most consumers correctly describe tariffs as a tax or a fee on imports, but many consumers are confused
Consumers are mixed on why the government sought to increase tariffs, with Gen Z’s being most favorable to higher tariffs having a positive impact on U.S. consumers
Shoppers are most optimistic about tariffs’ driving more U.S. made products, but accompanied by higher prices and a mixed impact on jobs
More than 40% of shoppers say that higher tariffs have affected their major purchase decisions so far

[Speaker notes] Full Service Restaurant - provides table service, where customers are seated by staff, orders are taken at the table, and food is delivered by servers. This category includes a wide range of establishments from casual dining (e.g., Applebee’s) to fine dining (e.g., Ruth’s Chris Steak House). 

Quick Service Restaurant (QSR) - industry term for what is commonly known as fast food. These establishments focus on speed, convenience, and affordability.

Fast Casual - bridge the gap between QSRs and Full Service Restaurants. They offer higher-quality food than QSRs, often with fresher ingredients and more customizable options, but without full table service. Examples include Chipotle, Panera Bread, and Shake Shack.

Retail Foodservice - refers to foodservice operations located within retail environments such as grocery stores, convenience stores, and big-box retailers. These can include prepared food sections, in-store cafes, or branded restaurant concepts within the retail space.

Inflation and recession are not direct opposites, but they are related. Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services, while a recession is a period of significant decline in economic activity.
