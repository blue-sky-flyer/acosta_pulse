## Slide 2

Objectives
The primary objective of this research was to uncover new shopper insights across a wide range of beverage categories, specifically exploring:
What’s Influencing Change - New and trending lifestyles and habits influencing beverage trends.
Perceptions - Discover shoppers’ perceptions of key types of beverages and brands.
Influencers and Motivations  - what are the top reasons for choosing each type of beverage
Barriers to Purchase  - what’s preventing shoppers from buying more of each type of beverage
Channels - Explore channel nuances, particularly foodservice and retail channels.

## Slide 3

Approach
The research was designed to assess the purchasing behavior of a wide range of beverage types.

## Slide 9

Most of the behavior changes revolve around the changes in the type of beverages consumed.
What jumps out at you about how or what you drink has changed over time?
“As I think back on how my beverage choices have changed, I notice I used to drink a lot more sugary juices and sodas when I was younger—like boxed fruit juices and soft drinks. As I’ve gotten older, I’ve started leaning more toward healthier options like bottled water, sparkling water, and hydration beverages like coconut water or Gatorade. I also drink more coffee now, especially specialty coffee when I'm out or working. One drink I don’t have much anymore is powdered drink mix-ins like Tang or Kool-Aid, which I used to love as a kid. These days, I’m interested in trying more probiotic drinks like kombucha and prebiotic sodas like Olipop, since I’ve heard they can support gut health.”   (Gen Z)
“I drink a larger variety I'm more adventurous with what I try and I'm also leaning towards healthier options. At more hydrating and “better for you” sodas things like that” (Millennial”)
“There are a lot of changes in how I drink as an adult, primarily having to do with sugar. I don't drink beverages with a lot of sugar. And when they do have sugar, they will have natural fruit sugars like in smoothies or juice, but I don't drink those as often. . If I drink soda, I will always drink diet. If I am ordering a specialty coffee drink, then I find ways to minimize the sugar content. For example, instead of adding a flavored syrup with sugar I will choose the sugar free option.”  (Boomer)
“I used to drink more soda. I love Coca-Cola, but I know it's bad for your health in the long run because of the high fructose corn syrup, so I switched to sparkling water so I can still get the "fizz" effect. It is refreshing, comes in so many flavors, and goes well with meals.  It can be fattening and has artificial coloring and high fructose corn syrup.” (Gen X)

## Slide 10

With their health goals a top priority, beverage drinkers are shifting towards options with health benefits in mind.
What jumps out at you about how or what you drink has changed over time?
“What jumps out is how much more intentional I’ve become about what I drink—focusing more on health, energy, and taste. As a child, I used to drink a lot of sugary sodas and artificially flavored juices like Tang or Ribena, but I’ve completely phased those out. Now, I’m more interested in drinking sparkling water, natural fruit juices, and occasionally kombucha for the probiotics. I’ve also developed a growing interest in herbal teas….” (Gen Z)
“I  pay more attention to what I drink and what benefits it has or additives it contains.  I am more careful about artificial colors and sweetness. I look for more health benefits such as minerals and probiotics.”  (Gen X)
“Have added Smart Water and protein drinks which I didn't drink when I was younger. No longer drinking Kool-Aid or Hawaiian Punch. i would like to try Kefir and add that to my routine….” (Boomer)
“Definitely a large shift away from sodas and replacing it with more water and water-adjacent drinks. It's probably a result of me wanted to curb my sugar consumption in a meaningful way. Well, it's definitely improved my overall energy level, and for now, has also resulted in some noticeable improvement in my physical health. But it's a long road for sustained gains; but progress is being made.”  (Millennial)
“I drink more protein- & electrolyte-based drinks for exercise and health purposes, as well as non-traditional sodas rather than traditional ones.  Healthier & better for working-out/exercise purposes. Non-traditional sodas also taste better.” (Boomer)

This research assesses shopper purchasing behavior for 31 different beverage types.
The study design set priorities on 25 of the 31 beverage types and collected insights on each beverage type using a standardized module of questions for the beverage type.
Note: some beverage types like coffee and tea had 2-3 additional questions added.
With N=2,487 and each respondent rating 2 beverage types, each beverage module will have ~N=200 respondents.
