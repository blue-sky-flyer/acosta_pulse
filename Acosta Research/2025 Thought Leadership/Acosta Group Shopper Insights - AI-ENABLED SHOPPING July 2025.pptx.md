## Slide 2

Study Objective
Shopper research conducted through Acosta Group’s proprietary Shopper Community
June 13 - 17, 2025
N = 1,074 U.S. Shoppers of least 1 retail channel in the last 3 months
To understand shopper awareness, interest, and current habits related to AI use and AI shopping assistants across products and services with emphasis on CPG and consumer electronics
[Speaker notes] Situation and Background
Brands and retail need to prepare for a shift in consumer behavior as AI begins to take an active role in how people shop. 
The emergence of AI-generated summaries in search results has already altered the way consumers gather product information. However, autonomous shopping agents are a bigger transformation. AI bots can find products and finalize purchases, including payments, without the shopper lifting a finger.
Instead of scrolling through ads or comparing product reviews, future consumers will rely on digital assistants to manage shopping lists, replenish household essentials, or to do the comparison shopping to select the best TV based on personal preferences. 

Criteria  
U.S. Rep - Adults 18+ who have shopped in at least 1 channel in the last 3 months

## Slide 3

Summary:  AI is beginning to take shopping to new levels – 		more intuitive, more personal, and more proactive
99% of consumers are aware of AI, and half claim to have used it
When asked to explain AI, shoppers describe it as a digital assistant that can learn and solve complex problems by mimicking human behavior and engaging in human-like conversation
Generative AI is already more widely used than many smart devices such as wearables and smart home devices
Gen Z has the highest AI adoption rate
53% of Gen Z Generative AI users feel it’s more trustworthy vs. other sources (vs. 26% Total Gen AI users)
With first mover advantage over major players, ChatGPT is the most used Gen AI tool, and has become a generic term – the Kleenex of AI
Besides search, 70% of shoppers have used one or more AI tools/features to help them shop (e.g., Gen AI, shopping apps, etc.)
62% of shoppers who use Gen AI to help them shop also use Gen AI for product search
While several major retailers have introduced AI shopping assistants, most shoppers are unaware of them, with < 15% usage rates
AI shopping tools are used more often for major purchases such as appliances and electronics along with baby care
Most shoppers haven’t heard of AI-Enabled Buying yet, but younger shoppers are the most interested in trying it
Few shoppers currently trust a Gen AI agent to buy items on their behalf due to concerns over privacy and unauthorized spending/fraud
When asked about specific categories, more shoppers are open to using a Gen AI agent to buy groceries than any other major category
Shoppers need transparency on how their data is stored and used in order to feel comfortable sharing their personal information with AI
Shoppers willing to share some information with AI are less likely to share personal details such as medical history or a photo, even if it helps personalization (e.g., for health information or help with cosmetics)

## Slide 4

Key Thought Starters / Implications
Source: AboutAmazon.com
AI is already changing how shoppers research and buy products
Traditional search and click is being replaced by conversations with Gen AI.
Personalization will move beyond past purchases to include lifestyle details such as household structure (e.g., kids, pets), activities, health and wellness concerns, and even seasonal needs to recommend the right products at the right time.
Multi-step purchases will feel like having a personal assistant: Say, “Alexa, we’re going to the beach this weekend. What should I bring?” She’ll ask follow-ups, suggest essentials, and build your cart - handling the details for the shopper.
Brand and retailer websites need to be optimized for AI
Sites provide the data and content needed to build trust, personalize experiences, enhance visibility in AI-powered search, and empower AI to deliver accurate and helpful responses. Brand sites can no longer simply be digital brochures.
Creating content tailored for keyword searches is not enough; content must be genuinely valuable, insightful conversation to rank in the AI world
Brands and retailers must prepare for zero-touch engagement as consumers begin to empower AI agents to make purchases on their behalf
Gen AI isn’t influenced by marketing.  AI agents rely on structured, credible signals:  optimized product pages, reviews, ratings, and third-party content - to prioritize brands.
As AI agents become the front door to shopping, brands must rethink how they show up in search, on the shelf, and everywhere shoppers convert.

## Slide 11

Source: Amazon, Kantar Retail - Global Innovation Trackers – 5/26/25 & 6/2/25

[Speaker notes] Kantar Retail – Global Innovation Tracker – 6/2/2025  -- Google example
Google’s AI Mode shopping experience combines Google’s Gemini generative AI chatbot with Google Shopping Graph, a real-time data set of products, inventory, and merchants with more than 50 billion listings and in-stock inventory data from a range of retailers.

Shopping Graph’s now has more than 50 billion product listings, each with details like reviews, prices, color options, and availability. More than 2 billion of those listings are refreshed hourly on Google.
In the coming months, shoppers will be able to search for products using conversational language and see a righthand panel of images and product listings personalized to their tastes that updates dynamically with relevant products and images as they go. 
Once shoppers choose a product, a new agentic AI checkout will help them buy at a price that fits their budget. Shoppers can tap “track price” on any product listing and set options like size or color and the amount they want to spend, and then receive price-drop notifications.
When they are ready to buy, shoppers can confirm the purchase details and tap “buy for me.” Behind the scenes, Google will add the item to their cart on the retailer’s site and securely complete the checkout with Google Pay. 
This agentic checkout feature will be rolling out in the coming months to product listings in the US.



Kantar Retail – Global Innovation Tracker – 5/26/2025
Walmart’s Sparky is a GenAI-powered shopping assistant integrated into the Walmart mobile app to enhance the online shopping experience through natural language interactions. 
Currently in beta testing, Sparky assists customers in finding products, comparing options, and making informed purchasing decisions by responding to conversational queries.
Sparky operates much like Amazon's Rufus, allowing natural language conversation within the shopping experience. It helps compare products and synthesize reviews and makes personalized recommendations.

## Slide 50

Alexa+: Shift From Reactive To Predictive Shopping
voice-activated shopping was the future. But, it never took off. Why? Because the consumer experience was awful.But now? That’s changing. Amazon just announced a major AI upgrade for Alexa. Alexa+ will be powered by generative AI. This will make voice assisted shopping more intuitive, more personal, and more proactive. - SHOPPING WILL BE SHAPED BY DAILY CONVERSATIONS, NOT JUST SEARCHES: If you ask Alexa for dinner ideas, she’ll start to learn your preferences. So later, when you say, “Alexa, add milk to my cart,” she’ll know you prefer oat milk.- RECOMMENDATIONS WILL BE SMARTER AND CONTEXT-AWARE: If you ask Alexa to set a reminder for your kid’s soccer game every Saturday, she’ll remember. So, weeks later, when you say, “Alexa, I need snacks for the team,” she’ll suggest bulk options that fit your preferences.- PERSONALIZATION WILL MOVE BEYOND PAST PURCHASES: Alexa will pick up on lifestyle details like who’s in your household, what activities you do, and even seasonal needs so she can recommend the right products at the right time.- MULTI-STEP PURCHASES WILL FEEL LIKE HAVING A PERSONAL ASSISTANT: Say, “Alexa, we’re going to the beach this weekend. What should I bring?” She’ll ask follow-ups, suggest essentials, and build your cart - handling the details so you don’t have to.- BUYING WILL BE PREDICTIVE, NOT REACTIVE: Instead of waiting for you to run out of coffee, Alexa will anticipate when you’ll need more based on past orders, how often your coffee maker runs, if you’ve got guests coming, and other conversations you’ve had. She’ll give you a heads-up before you even think about reordering.
