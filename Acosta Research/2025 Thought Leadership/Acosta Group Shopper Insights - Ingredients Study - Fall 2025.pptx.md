## Slide 2

To understand consumer perceptions of ingredients commonly found in food, beverages, and beauty products, with a focus on distinguishing between those considered "good" (e.g., natural sweeteners, plant-based) and "bad" (e.g., artificial additives, synthetic dyes, etc.).
AND
To understand consumer awareness and interest in FDA restrictions on synthetic food dyes and sweeteners in foods and the transition to natural alternatives.
Study Objectives
Shopper research conducted through Acosta Group’s proprietary Shopper Community

N = 1,083 U.S. Shoppers
[Speaker notes] Criteria  
U.S. Rep - Adults 18+ who do at least some of their households shopping for groceries and household needs

## Slide 3

Summary: Concerns over artificial ingredients in food and personal care products is not a fringe issue
Consumers are increasingly scrutinizing what goes into their food, drinks, and beauty products, with a strong preference for natural ingredients over artificial additives and synthetic dyes
While most people read labels before first trying an item, the frequency of reading labels varies significantly depending on shoppers’ nutrition habits
Shoppers are favorable to the Make America Healthy Again initiative (MAHA) that seeks to phase out harmful ingredients
Most people, including those who aren’t as concerned about ingredients or nutrition, believe that more food product safety regulation is needed
Most shoppers support MAHA’s stricter standards - even as they worry about potential price increases and changes in product taste
MAHA has prompted several manufacturers to replace artificial ingredients with natural alternatives ahead of potential regulations
Label reading is now a routine part of shopping, especially for those with allergies or sensitivities
Younger generations are leading the shift toward more frequent ingredient checks
Older shoppers tend to describe a healthy item as containing no “bad” ingredients, while younger shoppers also look for “natural” or “organic”
Artificial sweeteners are viewed with skepticism
While half of shoppers actively avoid added sugar in products they buy, 73% have tried one or more common artificial and natural sweeteners
Given their history of safety concerns, saccharin and aspartame are the sweeteners avoided by the most shoppers
When it comes to beauty and personal care, product performance outweighs ingredients
While more shoppers are concerned about food safety regulation, shoppers who prefer natural ingredients want more beauty product safety regulation
