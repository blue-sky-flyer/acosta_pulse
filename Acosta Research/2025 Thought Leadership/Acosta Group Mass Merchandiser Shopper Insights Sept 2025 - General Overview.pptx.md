## Slide 3

Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Sept 2025     [N=916]
2025 Mass Merchandiser Shopper Insights: Key Findings
Mass is an omnichannel go-to for routine tripsMost shoppers rely on weekly trips to Mass brick & mortar for groceries and essentials, with 1-in-4 also placing weekly online orders, blending physical and digital formats at each retailer.
Deals drive behaviorPromotions like BOGO and TPR are powerful motivators at Mass, with 86% of shoppers saying they have taken advantage of a good deal even when not needing the item(s) on promotion.
Mass shoppers cross Mass retailersShoppers are fluid in their retailer choices, with Walmart being the biggest beneficiary of cross-shopping. Half of Mass shoppers also shop Amazon, who wins on limitless assortment and free delivery.
Value is more than price, but price still leadsMass shoppers define value as low price without risking quality. Sales, discounts, and product durability also matter, reinforcing that value is about both cost and performance.
Apps are a trip companion
Apps are a key tool when accessed in-store to find deals, check prices, and new items. With 2-in-3 accessing apps to shop online, they are a vital touchpoint to engage and influence choices.
Exclusive spend in key categoriesShoppers often turn to Mass exclusively to buy certain staple foods (shelf stable, refrigerated) and household needs (cleaning, paper products).
Everyday price and convenience for the winShoppers are drawn to the ability to get everything from groceries to apparel in one trip, often at lower prices versus other stores. Broad assortment and good locations make Mass a practical and efficient choice.
Shoppers want moreGoing forward, broader assortments are the top shopper ask – namely, expansion of ethnic foods, better-for-you options, more apparel, auto care and home improvement tools.

## Slide 4

Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Sept 2025     [N=916]
2025 Mass Merchandiser Shopper Insights: Now What?
Make the one-stop trip easierShoppers love the convenience of getting everything in one place, a concept Mass retailers can continuously highlight in shopper marketing. Additionally, promoting essentials – like groceries, household items, and personal care – together and creating logical adjacencies supports a one-stop experience.
Lean into digital toolsShoppers access their retailer apps for more than just online shopping – apps are a key part of the trip and real-time decision-making. Retailers can turn apps into a ‘Smart’ shopping companion, helping shoppers find deals, navigate the store, announce new items – making it a means of communication and engagement with their shoppers vs. just a tool.
Expand and highlight assortment where it mattersShoppers are asking for more – ethnic foods, better-for-you options, and apparel, so Mass retailers must ensure assortment reflects evolving needs. Interestingly, more consumers shop at Mass for the variety of products than for their store brands; however, most Mass shoppers are open to store brands – retailers may use them as an entry vehicle for new categories.
Watch for cross-shopping and win them backAmazon, Walmart, and Target are favored retailers of Mass shoppers. To stem leakage, retailers can defend on value and price perception as well as differentiated trip experiences, especially in-store where top competitor Amazon cannot replicate.
Make the store experience countMass shoppers value the fundamentals: clean and organized stores, fair prices for quality products, and classic deals like BOGO. Retailers should also leverage personalization – unique deals, product recommendations, localized assortment alerts based on preferences – to create a trip experience that feels tailored and meaningful. In-store services like pharmacy, food counters, and branded spaces (Starbucks, Ulta, etc.) add value – retailers should highlight these as part of the one-stop shop trip, not just extras.
