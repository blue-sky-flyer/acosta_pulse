## Slide 3

Summary:  Compared to the 2025 study, AI is moving beyond experimentation to a part of everyday shopping
AI is now mainstream: 99% of shoppers are aware of AI, and over half have used it
Google is driving most new growth and is rolling out a universal shopping cart feature to allow shopping across retailers in the platform
AI tools are influencing purchase decisions, especially among younger shoppers (e.g., Gen AI, shopping apps, etc.)
Shoppers leveraging AI tools are more likely to stick to a familiar brand than to buy a new brand that AI recommends
Purchase decisions are being driven by AI tools’ ability to compare options, identify deals, and simplify decision-making
39% of shoppers who use AI tools or features are doing so while shopping in stores, including 55% of Gen Z
Gen AI is becoming a habit with more penetration than that for smart home devices and wearables
34% of shoppers use Gen AI for shopping, 44% of Gen AI users use it daily, and 75% have used it for product search
Gen AI shoppers use AI for in-depth exploration and evaluation, but turn to traditional search and retailer apps for quick or simple tasks
As in 2025, most Gen AI users find it as trustworthy as other sources with trust highest among younger shoppers
Gen AI shoppers generally consider AI recommendations as a starting point, and wrong or misleading data significantly damages trust
Shoppers still need transparency on how their data is stored and used to feel comfortable sharing their personal information with Gen AI
Younger shoppers willing to share information with Gen AI are more open to sharing personal details such as medical history or a photo to aid personalization than older shoppers
Most see value in a Gen AI shopping agent that remembers their details, but comfort levels vary depending on control and transparency

## Slide 4

Key Thought Starters / Implications
As AI becomes a primary tool for product discovery and evaluation, the winning brands and retailers will be those that are most visible to AI, most useful to shoppers, and most trusted with consumer data.
Win early in the decision journey as consumers increasingly rely on AI to discover, evaluate, and select products
Traditional search and click is being replaced by conversations with Gen AI especially when shoppers are uncertain about what to buy
However, search still has a place for simple requests such as product lookup or price comparisons
Brand and retailer websites need to be optimized for AI
Tailoring content for keyword searches is not enough as content must be genuinely valuable to be visible to AI
Shopper and product attributes must be embedded into digital content
Product data, pricing, and inventory must be accurate and consistent across all platforms
Make personalization worth the data exchange
Consumers are becoming more willing to share information when they see a clear benefit
Brands and retailers that provide transparency and control over data usage will be better positioned to build trust and loyalty

Most people see real value in an AI shopping assistant that remembers their details, but comfort levels vary widely depending on control and transparency.
The key concern: transparency and choice. People want to know exactly what’s being tracked and why.

The Helpful Side
Many participants appreciate how remembering past purchases, budgets, and dietary needs saves time and makes shopping easier. One person said it feels very helpful because the AI learns their preferences and provides personalized recommendations without them having to repeat information. Another noted that for someone with disabilities or dietary restrictions, having an AI look out for them feels comforting and helps them live independently.

People also value the practical benefits. An AI that knows dietary needs can flag potential allergens in dishes. One that tracks budget helps people stay on track financially. Several participants mentioned they love not having to keep feeding the same information repeatedly.

The Uncomfortable Middle Ground
Many fall somewhere between helpful and creepy. They like the convenience but worry about what happens to their data. One person said it feels helpful for shopping but uncomfortable because they’re unsure what gets saved or how it’s used. Another explained they’re fine with it only if they have control—they want to see, change, or delete what the AI remembers.

Creep Factor

Since Gen AI keeps learning more about you to improve recommendations, when would it start to feel like it knows too much and start to feel creepy or “big brother” to you?

People draw the line on creepy AI in several clear ways. Most worry about AI knowing things they never shared, tracking them across websites, or using personal details to push products. A few feel uncomfortable from the start but accept it anyway, while some say they'd only feel crossed if AI forced them into decisions.

Add a Nike sneaker from Search, a Sephora moisturizer from a Gemini conversation, a Wayfair desk from a YouTube review. The Retailer Position Looks Worse Than The Marketing Implies
The official line is that retailers stay the merchant of record, keep customer relationships, and gain a new acquisition surface. That framing is technically accurate but strategically incomplete.

When discovery, cart management, price monitoring, compatibility checking, loyalty surfacing and checkout authorization all live inside Google, the retailer's primary leverage over the customer, the cart itself, is no longer theirs. The merchant of record stays the merchant of record. The merchant of decision becomes Google. Those are not the same thing.


Final Thought
For brands, the playbook narrows. Two priorities. First, get the product feed and structured data into the state Google needs (the AEO and ACO work covered in Edition 23). Without that, you don't surface inside Universal Cart, full stop. Second, ask harder questions of your retail media spend. If a meaningful share of considered purchases starts to happen inside Google rather than on retailer DTC or marketplace sites, the relative value of a Walmart Connect or Roundel placement shifts. 

For retailers, the strategic question is bigger. Universal Cart is a deal you can't really refuse, because the alternative is being invisible to a billion shopping queries a day. But the terms of that deal will tighten over time. Loyalty integration, in-cart ads, agent-driven price negotiation, AP2-mediated subscription flows. Every layer Google adds is a layer Google charges for, advertises against, or monetizes through preferred merchant tiers. The retailers who win the next five years will treat Universal Cart as a distribution channel, not a partnership, and will build their own conversational and agentic infrastructure underneath it.
