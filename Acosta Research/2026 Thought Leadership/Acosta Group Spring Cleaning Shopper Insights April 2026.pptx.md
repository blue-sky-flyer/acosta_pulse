## Slide 3

Spring cleaners take on more tasks across the home, including decluttering and organization, but rely on familiar routines, products, and tools. The focus is on doing more, not cleaning differently.
Despite the expanded scope of spring cleaning, shopping behavior stays rooted in familiar retailers, modest budgets, and trusted brands.
Shoppers broadly agree on what “green” means but differ in confidence and evaluation, with spring cleaners showing greater engagement and openness to green cleaning products.
Legacy national brands serve as default spring cleaning anchors, while greener or emerging options are layered in by task. Shoppers seek incremental improvements without sacrificing effectiveness.
Spring cleaning is widely adopted and emotionally resonant, with nearly 8‑in‑10 households participating. Shoppers view the occasion as both a functional task and a psychological reset.

## Slide 4

Spring cleaners prioritize effectiveness, residue, and price. Sustainability and “green” claims matter, but only when performance expectations are met. Safety for children and pets is a concern.
Trust is an important component, and clear, understandable ingredient stories help validate choices, especially for households with children.
Receptivity to concentrates, refills, and multipurpose products shows that shoppers will adjust behavior when benefits are clear, saving time, space, or money without added complexity.
With some shoppers not noticing spring cleaning promotions (1-in-5), visibility and clarity at shelf and online are critical. The opportunity lies in product and packaging clearly aiding decision making.
Brands can leverage the spring-cleaning occasion by upgrading familiar products, maintaining efficacy while improving scent, ingredients, or convenience.
