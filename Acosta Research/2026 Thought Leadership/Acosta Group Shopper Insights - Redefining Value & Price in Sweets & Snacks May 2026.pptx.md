## Slide 2

BACKGROUND
INDUSTRY ISSUE
Price matters more than ever, but so does perception. As economic pressure persists, shoppers are continually redefining what “value” means across candy and snacks, prompting brands and retailers to rethink pricing strategies in real time.
BUSINESS QUESTIONS:
How are consumers responding to price in candy and snacks categories?
How do perceptions and sensitivity to pricing vary by the consumer or the occasion?
What are preferences around branded and private label products?
Where are price gaps widening and how are “walk-away” thresholds changing?
What does it take to protect loyalty and margin in a volatile market?
RESEARCH OBJECTIVES
To narrow scope of study, research will focus on
salty snacks and chocolate.
Understand current category engagement and purchase patterns (e.g., frequency, product-type, where shopped)
Identify how key drivers influences consumer perception across categories and drive purchase behavior (e.g., social media, store placement, private vs branded label, etc.)
Explore key levers that affect purchase behavior / category perception: social media, natural / organic / functional foods, private label vs branded, and seasonal affects
Deep-dive into two key area products: chocolate bars & popcorn
Measure brand awareness -> brand purchase among shoppers
Identify price value perceptions in category across everyday, premium, and trending products
Explore how shopper influences (e.g., social media) affect willingness to pay
Source: Acosta’s Group Shopper Community - Sweets & Snacks, March 2026

## Slide 4

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
When it comes to chocolate and salty snacks, shoppers have higher price thresholds for higher quality/premium products.
Those who are open to paying more for include: younger shoppers, those who are on social media (particularly Instagram and TikTok), and those who have previously purchased ‘trending’ brands
Both chocolate and salty snacks have more shoppers claiming to have bought more often than less often in the past 6 months
Increased purchase frequency among both categories were driven by younger generations (Gen Z + Millennials)
Among chocolate shoppers, those with higher household incomes also drove increased frequency
Whether chocolate or salty snacks, shoppers are primarily influenced to purchase by previous familiarity with product, its reputation, packaging size, and promotions / discounts available
Among chocolate shoppers specifically, limited or seasonal offers also moved the needle on influence
Among salty snack shoppers, natural and health benefits played a larger role in influencing purchase decisions
Source: Acosta’s Group Shopper Community - Sweets & Snacks, March 2026

## Slide 5

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Salty Snack Shopper Specifics
Outside of mass merchandisers and traditional grocery, warehouse club, dollar stores, and discount stores are sought out by a third of shoppers.
Natural / organic / clean ingredients, as well as health benefits, play a large role in what shoppers are looking for when it comes to choosing salty snacks.
Among health-minded shoppers, high protein and reduced sugar are sought after options in addition to better-for-you ingredients.
While salty snack shoppers are not specifically seeking out salty snacks on social media, they are sometimes driven to buy a salty snack so they can participate in a trend or try a product due to the ‘fear of missing out.’
Source: Acosta’s Group Shopper Community - Sweets & Snacks, March 2026

## Slide 6

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Chocolate Shopper Specifics
Outside of mass merchandisers and traditional grocery, other key channels including dollar stores, drug stores, and convenience stores play a uniquely large role in where shoppers search for chocolate, with dollars stores coming in at #3.
Chocolate shoppers see chocolate as an everyday indulgence.  It may seem counter-intuitive, but they often correlate ‘healthier’ chocolate options (such as better-for-you ingredients) with better tasting chocolate (not sacrificing taste for chocolate but rather improving it).
While chocolate shoppers are not specifically seeking out chocolate on social media, they discover new chocolate products on social and admit to impulse purchasing due to social media.
Source: Acosta’s Group Shopper Community - Sweets & Snacks, March 2026

## Slide 27

DETERMINING SHOPPER’S PRICING THRESHOLD
Once a shoppers was assigned to a grouping, their willingness to pay for each category (whether they had purchase previously or were a prospective shopper) was determined through a methodology called:
The Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter
Measures a shopper’s price sensitivity
Provides an optimal price point (OPP) for product
Defines a shopper’s accepted range of prices
Identifies when a product is perceived as:
Too cheap to purchase (e.g., quality questioned)
A good price for the value
A little expensive for the value
Too expensive to purchase
Acceptable Price Range
Getting to expensive to Purchase for most shoppers
Getting so cheap shoppers begin to question why
Optimal
price
point
Source: Acosta’s Group Shopper Community - Sweets & Snacks, March 2026
[Speaker notes] Note: when explaining to audience be sure to highlight that this is SHOPPER’s willingness to pay and does not include other factors of the product such as costs to produce. The Van Westendorp can help to determine if a product or product category can be successful at its current price offering or if there is room to optimize. Other factors, such as a shopper’s generation, brand reputation, product’s social media presence, etc may be additional levers that determine a products success at a certain price point and a shift a shopper’s willingness to pay in either direction. 

Van Westendorp Questions: 
VW1. At what price would you begin got think the item is so inexpensive that you would question the quality and not consider it (in USD $0.00)? 

VW2. At what price would you think the item is a bargain - a great buy for the money (in USD $0.00)? 

VW3. At what price would you think the item is getting expensive, but you still might consider it (in USD $0.00)? 

VW4. At what price would you begin to think the item is too expensive to consider (in USD $0.00)?

## Slide 32
