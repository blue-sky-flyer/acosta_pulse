## Slide 4

The Why? Behind The BuyTM Key Findings
Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Jan 2026     [N=1,800]
Traditional Grocery and Mass are still the core of U.S. shopping, but Value and Online grew fastest | Shoppers continued to rely most on Traditional Grocery and Mass, reinforcing these stores as “trip anchors.” But value‑driven and digital formats, including Value Grocery, Online‑Only, Non‑Grocery Online, Grocery Online, and Club. They had the strongest trip growth driven by better prices, assortment, and convenience.
Affordability pressures shaped nearly every shopping behavior | Shoppers felt “generally comfortable” covering grocery costs, but over half reported stress about affording groceries at some point, leaning heavily into budgeting habits: waiting for deals, cutting back on non‑essentials, and trading down to lower‑cost products.
Deal‑seeking became a routine | Shoppers head into trips with a deal‑first mindset. Many checked ads, apps, and digital coupons to confirm they weren’t missing savings. In store, straightforward deals like TPR and BOGO were most influential, reinforcing that instant, no‑friction savings matter most.
Online shopping continued to grow, but for most, it complemented rather than replaced in‑store | Nearly one‑third of shoppers did most/all 2025 grocery shopping online, but majority still shop primarily in stores and use online for convenience or fill‑in. Shoppers told us what they value most about online is clarity and efficiency: strong product details, good filters, saved lists, and loyalty program integration.
Freshness, quality, and strong store brands define a “great” store | Shoppers consistently pointed to produce and meat/seafood as top indicators of a quality grocery store. Strong store brands ranked highly, aligning with the notion that affordability alone isn’t enough; shoppers want quality without trading down their standards. Store environment expectations remained grounded in basics: clean stores, helpful staff, well‑organized aisles, and convenient locations.

## Slide 5

The Why? Behind The BuyTM Key Findings (cont.)
Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Jan 2026     [N=1,800]
Physical stores still win on browsing, inspiration, and ability to inspect | Shoppers who prefer physical stores say it’s because they can see, touch, and select products themselves, discover new items, and get the immediate gratification of taking items home right away. Many enjoy browsing for ideas and checking clearance sections, which online environments struggle to replicate.
Out-of-stocks are a
disruptive friction point | Most shoppers blame retailers for out-of-stock issues, and repeated unavailability can cause lost interest in both retailers and brands. Shoppers want simple transparency: real‑time inventory, notifications, and meaningful substitutes or discounts when items are unavailable.
Store brands continued gaining momentum as shoppers balanced cost and quality | Nearly all shoppers buy store brands, and over one‑quarter purchased more than the year prior, citing strong quality, comparable taste, and budget pressures.Shoppers typically compare store brands with name brands before choosing, reinforcing that the choice is intentional, not automatic.
AI entered the grocery trip, mainly as a value-seeking tool | About one‑third of shoppers used AI‑enabled tools to find deals, check product details, or compare prices. While still emerging, 14% expect to use AI more often going forward, indicating growing comfort with tech‑supported decision‑making.
Restaurant food spending has softened with the increase in menu pricing | Most shoppers noticed higher restaurant and takeout/delivery prices and responded by cutting back, choosing lower‑cost options, skipping the extras, or trading full‑service meals for smaller treats like coffee or bakery items. Many expect dining‑out frequency to stay the same or decline as higher prices outside-the-home will continue to affect at‑home meal decisions.

The Why? Behind the BuyTMCHANNELS & RETAILERS SHOPPEDKey Findings
Per The Shopper…

Traditional Grocery and Mass remain the dominant go‑to channels
Traditional Grocery (79%) and Mass (73%) are the most widely shopped, reinforcing their role as trip anchors for shoppers.
Trip growth was strongest in value‑oriented and online channels
Shoppers increased trips to Grocery Online, Value Grocery, Online‑Only (e.g., Amazon, Chewy), Non‑Grocery Online, and Club, driven mainly by better prices, strong value perception, availability, and new items.
Trip declines were concentrated in non‑food channels.
Office Supplies, Electronics, Home Improvement, Beauty, and Drug saw the steepest trip declines, often due to price pressures or reduced need. Shoppers may be consolidating trips into fewer retailers and possibly deprioritizing categories sold there. 
Retailer shopping skews reflect channel strengths.
Across all retailers shopped, Walmart, Kroger, Costco, Amazon, Aldi, and Dollar formats emerged as top‑ranked destinations, aligning with shoppers’ focus on value, convenience, and broad assortment. 
Distinct demographics are driving channel growth and decline.
Trip increases were fueled by specific groups: High Income for Grocery Online, Gen Z/Millennials for Online‑Only, Hispanics for Value Grocery, and families for Club.    Boomers and No‑Kid households drove declines at Electronics, Office Supplies, and Drug.
