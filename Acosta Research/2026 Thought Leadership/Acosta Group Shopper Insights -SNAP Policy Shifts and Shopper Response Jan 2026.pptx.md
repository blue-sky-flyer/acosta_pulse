## Slide 3

Summary
Nearly 1 in 8 U.S. residents are SNAP recipients who collectively represent $245 billion in purchasing power
SNAP households average 30% share of $ sales across CPG aisles and over-index on spending vs. non-SNAP households
However, market share from SNAP households has declined across departments and restaurants
SNAP participants describe SNAP as a lifeline that they depend on to supplement their incomes
During the 2025 federal government shutdown, those who didn’t get their full SNAP benefits got help from community organizations and relied on family and friends or resources they had on hand to get by until benefits were restored
No surprise, SNAP recipients say that value is most important to them when grocery shopping
However, nearly half of SNAP shoppers say it’s worth paying more for high quality brands and convenience when shopping for CPG
Despite tight budgets, significantly more SNAP recipients see better value in name brands vs. shoppers who have never been on SNAP
SNAP recipients spend 75% of their food and beverage dollars on national brands
SNAP product restrictions may cut US sales by up to 4% in key categories
18 states so far have USDA approval to restrict SNAP benefits’ usage on soda, candy, certain snacks, etc., but specifics vary by state
The retail impact will grow as more states restrict SNAP dollars as SNAP HHs in current waiver states make up over 20% of candy and soft drink sales
SNAP recipients have a mixed outlook for 2026 with 1-in-10 not sure what the year will bring

Implications for Manufacturers & Retailers 

Manufacturers: 
Reformulate and reposition products to meet SNAP’s new eligibility. 
Tailor pack sizes and pricing to maintain or increase affordability. 
Collaborate with retailers on compliant assortment and promotional strategies. 

Retailers: 
Optimize value product messaging to SNAP-eligible consumers 
Enhance merchandising to reflect SNAP shopper preferences in both value retailers and in other retailers with sizable SNAP customer bases. 
Leverage shopper insights to target promotions that resonate with value-seeking households, especially those impacted by benefit changes

## Slide 27

Implications:  SNAP policy changes could shift shopper demand and basket composition
Retail Operations
Foot traffic at value-focused banners and discount formats may fluctuate.
Retailers in high SNAP density areas may need to adjust inventory and pricing strategies.
Loyalty and personalization programs may need recalibration to reflect new shopper segments.
CPG Strategy
Brands may need to reassess pack sizes, pricing tiers, and promotional cadence.
Opportunity to highlight value messaging and nutrition forward products.
Consider collaborative program with retailers to support affected shoppers (e.g., coupons, meal kits, digital education).
Analytics & Forecasting
Monitor SNAP redemption trends and category-level shifts closely.
Use store-level data to identify emerging patterns in SNAP heavy geographies.
Prepare for regional variability based on how states implement administrative changes.
