## Slide 4

2026 FIFA World Cup Pre-Event Implications
Keep In Mind…
Source: Acosta Group Shopper Community Survey, Spring 2026
Plan for a multi‑occasion event, not single moment.
World Cup drives engagement across weeks, creating multiple viewing, shopping, and hosting occasions.
Food is the primary connective tissue of the World Cup viewing experience.
Familiar, shareable game‑day foods anchor their viewing behavior, while global flavors and team‑themed elements add differentiation.
Viewing will extend to social and public spaces.
Younger viewers and families push viewing beyond the home, expanding the range of food, beverage, and on‑premise opportunities.
Most World Cup shopping is intentional, not impulsive.
Purchases are largely layered into routine or planned trips, so visibility and inspiration matter most before match days.
Displays must balance excitement with ease and value.
Visual impact draws attention, but convenience, bundling, and clear pricing convert into purchase.
The World Cup is both a sports and cultural occasion. Emotional drivers, such as celebration, connection, and tradition, are as important as competition, especially for Hispanic and younger audiences.

## Slide 13

Fan type creates a clear intensity gradient, from planned, high‑frequency viewing to selective participation, with superfans and loyal supporters more likely to watch multiple matches a week.
The World Cup blends heritage, culture, and community, broadening its appeal beyond traditional sports. Boomers are drawn to prestige and tradition, while Gen Z is motivated by global diversity.
Hispanic viewers over‑index on hosting and trying new foods or drinks and are more likely to associate the World Cup with celebration (66%), social connection (66%), and tradition (55%).
While nearly all viewers will watch at home, half plan to watch outside out of home, including bars, restaurants, or in public.  Gen Z and Millennials are more likely to watch in public settings.
Nearly 7-in-10 viewers expect to watch most or nearly all soccer matches, with 9-in-10 watching the knockout stage as well as the final game.

## Slide 24

Across audiences, World Cup food plans center on shareable staples, with over 60% associating the event with snacks, comfort foods, and alcoholic beverages that reinforce ease and familiarity.
While classic game‑day foods anchor the occasion, 46% associate the World Cup with global or international flavors, highlighting cultural experimentation without replacing core staples.
Nearly 8‑in‑10 shoppers plan to purchase food or beverages for the World Cup, with 48% doing so as part of routine shopping and another 29% stocking up early.
7‑in‑10 shoppers say themed displays influence their purchasing, with World Cup–themed and cross‑category displays outperforming checkout or siloed placements.
Planning and food‑forward roles dominate World Cup behavior, with more than 4-in-10 identifying as planners or foodies, compared to just 1-in-10 last‑minute improvised shoppers.
