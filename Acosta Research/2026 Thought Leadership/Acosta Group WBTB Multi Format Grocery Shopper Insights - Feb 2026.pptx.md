## Slide 16

Why TRADITIONAL Over Other Grocery Formats
MGF shoppers tell us why they choose Traditional Grocery OVER other Grocery types…
Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Jan 2026     [Multi-Grocery Format Shoppers N=997]
Consistently delivers strong value through competitive everyday pricing, good promos, and loyalty programs that meaningfully reduce total spend.
#1 PRICE & VALUE
Easy-to-access and close-to-home or work allow for full trips fast. Ability to consolidate needs into one trip make it convenient for routine to quick trips.
#2 CONVENIENCE
Broad assortments and name-brand availability make it a dependable one-stop where shoppers know they’ll find what they need.
#3 SELECTION
Associated with fresher and superior Produce, better-cut Meats, reliable Bakery and Deli, Traditional is a trustworthy source of quality fresh foods.
#4 FRESHNESS
Traditional is often selected for products –premium, national‑
brands, and specialty – that shoppers say they can’t reliably find elsewhere.
#5 SPECIFIC ITEMS

Why GROCERY ONLINE Over Other Grocery Formats
MGF shoppers tell us why they choose a Grocery Retailer’s website/app OVER brick-&-mortar Grocery…
Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, Jan 2026     [Multi-Grocery Format Shoppers N=997]
Significant reduction in trip time and effort – can build carts quickly, reorder past items, avoid crowds, and shop anytime.
#1 CONVENIENCE
Enhances deal‑seeking by making promos more visible, simplifies loyalty usage and comparing prices across stores, and easy access to digital coupons.
#2 PRICE & VALUE
Easy web navigation, personal reco’s, and ability to edit orders and track substitutions improve accuracy and lend to shopper confidence.
#3 EXPERIENCE
Improved quality control has increased trust, as shoppers note positive experiences with order accuracy, especially on fresh items.
#4 ORDER QUALITY
Flexible delivery and curbside options are major drivers of online grocery adoption, especially on bulk orders.
#5 DELIVERY

Key Themes for MGF Shoppers “Go To” Grocery Channels
How Shoppers Split FOOD Trips
Fresh is anchored in Traditional. For Fresh perimeter, shoppers default to Traditional Grocery, the clear “go‑to” for Produce, Meats and Seafood – signaling trust in the channel’s core fresh departments.
Dairy splits. Traditional is first choice for refrigerated Dairy foods and beverages – but Value is a strong second, suggesting shoppers will trade channels on staples.
Natural/Specialty is a “clean label” destination. Traditional is a distant second, as diet standards steer most trips toward specialty banners.
Local/Ethnic owns cultural authenticity. Local/Ethnic is the first stop for authentic, imported brands and specialized produce/proteins.
Snacks & Non-Dairy Beverages skew Value after Traditional. Everyday center‑store Snacks & Sweets and non-Dairy beverages lean Traditional, then Value and Online for price and convenience – implying stock‑ups happen in familiar aisles and are filled in at lower‑cost channels.
Perimeter store-made is Traditional’s advantage. Store‑prepped meals and Bakery/Deli are primarily picked up at Traditional, with Natural/Specialty and Local/Ethnic being credible second choices.
“Specialty food” is twofold. For premium/unique Specialty Foods, shoppers look to Natural/Specialty and Local/Ethnic – the two formats most associated with discovery, limited runs, and hard‑to‑find items.
How Shoppers Split NON-FOOD Trips
Online is now in line with Traditional on home care staples. Grocery .coms are on par with Traditional on Paper Products, Cleaners, Laundry and Storage (garbage and food storage bags, foil) needs, signaling that bulky auto‑replenish items have shifted.
HBC also shifts digital. Hair Care and Oral Care show Online as the #1 or co‑#1 go‑to and Traditional second, as shoppers find appeal in saved lists, substitutions, and delivery for planned, repeatable basket.
OTC is a need vs. convenience split. Health care is split between Traditional and Online, suggesting shoppers may be balancing OTC buys between immediate need and the convenience/assortment of .coms.
Vitamins/Supplements are digital first. Online (where shoppers can research and access a wider assortment) leads, but Natural/Specialty (trusted for quality and health) remains an important secondary.
Baby & Pet underline the replenishment model. Baby Needs skew Online over Traditional, suggesting subscription‑like behavior. Pet Needs are led by Traditional and Online, with Value a strong price player.
Value plays the everyday alternative. Across Non-Foods, Value Grocery is a consistent second/third choice – likely attracting the shoppers who focus on price and are flexible on brand, especially for commodities.
