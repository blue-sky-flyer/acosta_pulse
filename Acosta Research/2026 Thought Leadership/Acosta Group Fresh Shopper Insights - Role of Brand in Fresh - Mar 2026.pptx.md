## Slide 4

Key Highlights:  Brand plays different roles in Fresh, shaping confidence and value
Fresh choices are driven first by fundamentals, freshness, quality, and price, but brand shapes consideration and confidence, particularly when shoppers compare trade‑offs between options.
Brand matters, but brand influence varies by category:
Some categories (e.g., refrigerated pasta and cheese) are brand‑essential, where national brands show strong impact on value and preference, influencing choice.
Others (e.g., fresh bread) are less brand-driven, with choice influenced more by in-store and product cues, such as freshness and appearance.
National brands and store brands work together to strengthen department perception; the presence of familiar national brands consistently reinforces trust and credibility across Fresh departments.
Incremental reach from brand may appear modest at an aggregate level but is concentrated in specific categories and shopper segments (e.g., National Brand Seekers, Millennials) where brand importance, relative lift, and economic value are meaningfully higher.
Bottom Line:  In Fresh, brand wins not by overpowering fundamentals, but by reinforcing trust, shaping value through quality/taste/appeal, and concentrating impact where and when it matters most.
[Speaker notes] This study shows that brand doesn’t play a single role in fresh—but when it matters, it creates outsized value.

The key insight is not just that “brand comes up a lot.”
The deeper insight is contextual dependence:
When shoppers are asked to advise someone else—especially in a moment of uncertainty—they revert to brand as a guidepost or safety net.


Original Headline:  When asked to advise a friend, many Fresh shoppers offer advice related to finding the right value equation with a balance of brand, freshness and quality ingredients. 

“What’s interesting here is not that brand is mentioned—it’s when it’s mentioned. When shoppers are asked to rank factors for themselves, brand competes with freshness, quality, and price. But when they’re asked to advise someone else—especially a friend—brand emerges as a shortcut for confidence. It’s the ‘if you’re unsure, go with a brand you trust’ response.”

Brand may not win every trade‑off—but when confidence is required, shoppers still tell others to trust brand.
