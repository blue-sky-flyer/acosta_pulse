## Slide 4

Executive Summary - Summertime Personal CareWhat’s Next for Brands and Retailers?
Source: Acosta Group Shopper Community Survey, May 2026
For RETAILERS…
Category triggers are not always seasonal. Suncare sets should stay up as long as possible, adjusting for weather and regional demand instead of the calendar. Shopping habits indicate that demand extends beyond Summer (with over 75% continuing to buy post–Labor Day), supporting set and display extension into early Fall. As noted for Brands, ‘need states’ messaging (e.g., travel, sports) can drive impulse and overall conversion.
Simplify the search with one destination. Shoppers may expect multiple placements but prefer one easy-to-find organized set or display. Sets should be timely, consistently stocked, and well-placed – to make it easy for the shopper and encourage conversion. BUT…
…if there IS to be secondary placement, make it strategic. Routine items stay in-aisle while exciting Suncare goes in seasonal destinations, endcaps, or store pharmacy (aligns with functional care).
Stock according to solution. Grouping Suncare, After-Sun Care, SPF levels, etc. together reflect how shoppers use and shop them.
Do not forget Digital. Shoppers note online research precedes their summer personal care. Support the journey from research to in-store discovery with quick re-ordering and consistent product availability.
For BRANDS…
Shopper messaging around activities first – not seasons. With purchases most often triggered by weather changes, away-from-home activities, and travel plans, messaging and merchandising should align with these behaviors.
Messaging should also highlight performance and value. With efficacy and price equally important to these shoppers, messaging that demonstrates a product’s functional benefits and value has the best chance at driving conversion.
Ensure product positioning supports year-round usage! A strong interest in hydrating and skincare benefits suggests an opportunity for Suncare to be part of a daily routine versus a seasonal need.
Let ‘current routines’ drive innovation. As over half of shoppers rely on habit to guide purchase decisions for summertime personal care, allow it to drive ideation so that new items fit into current usage – such as SPF in everyday skincare, or hydration in tanning products.
Consider usage differences across key shopper segments. Younger shoppers tend to use different products for different body areas, while older shoppers prefer simple routines. Brands have opportunity to support more targeted forms or benefit-driven products.

Source: Acosta Group Shopper Community Survey, May 2026
Summer personal care routines are anchored in core, habit-driven products, but shopper behavior varies by category, timing, and need. While hair removal/shaving remains routine and consistent, suncare introduces more variability in purchase timing, brand choice, and shopping behavior.
Summer usage is widespread and routine for deodorant (85%), hair removal products (79%), and sunscreen (71%).  And while all three are used more during the summer, sunscreen and insect repellant and treatment have the most significant seasonal increases.
