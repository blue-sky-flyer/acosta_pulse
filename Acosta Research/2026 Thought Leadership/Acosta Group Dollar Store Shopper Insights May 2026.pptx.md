## Slide 4

Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, May 2026     [Dollar Store Shoppers N=1,132]
Dollar remains a high-frequency mission-driven channel. Most shoppers make trips monthly, with many paying a weekly visit, and nearly half using Dollar specifically for budget-conscious trips. While not a replacement for Grocery, Dollar is a supplemental and needs-based channel, often used for “fill in” needs.
‘Value’ is Dollar’s core driver, but this perception is under pressure. While more than half report increasing Dollar trips due to rising prices elsewhere, shoppers also noticed price increases at Dollar as well – meaning, the channel’s key benefit may be starting to erode.
Dollar is strongest in life and home essentials and less in premium and health-forward categories, making Dollar a destination for convenience and basics vs. a full grocery solution.
Execution is a barrier to Dollar’s growth.Nearly 40% of shoppers say they avoid certain dollar stores, agreeing on top reasons being out-of-stocks, cluttered/unclean stores, and poor staffing that affects how stores run.
Assortment gaps may limit the shopper from trading up or expanding trips. Premium natural/organic options at Dollar are not only limited but have lingering trust concerns if sold at a dollar store. This is holding Dollar back from building larger baskets.
Shoppers say they want Dollar to evolve, but NOT at the expense of value. They have noticed the channel’s expansion efforts this year, with new items and more seasonal assortments, but continue to define it by its core role of being the “value-driven quick trip.”

## Slide 5

Acosta Group Shopper Community survey, May 2026     [Dollar Store Shoppers N=1,132]
2026 The Dollar Store Shopper
So What?
For BRANDS…
Item sold at dollar stores should speak to how people shop Dollar: quick fill-ins for immediate needs. Smaller sizes (with prices reflective of less volume) and grab-&-go can win.
Deliver value that feels real, not just cheap. Shoppers will try lower-priced options, but only if the quality feels ‘good enough’ to trust.
Focus on core categories – snacks, beverages, pantry, and household. These center-store home essentials consistently drive traffic and repeat purchasing.
Help close the trust gap, especially in higher-risk categories. Quality cues and simple messaging about how items are a value to the shopper can make a difference.
Lean into “better-for-you” but keep it approachable and affordable. Shoppers tell us there is demand for this, but only if it fits within shoppers’ value expectations.
For RETAILERS…
Get ‘the basics’ right first. The friction points driving shoppers away are operational. Clean stores, full shelves, and ample staff matters more than stores may realize.
Do not lose the edge on ‘value,’ something this channel has above others. Even small changes that threaten this perception will cause channel shifting.
Dollar needs to be reliably stocked on everyday needs. If key items are not in stock, shoppers will simply leave. There is less channel loyalty here.
Store brands may play a role in building trust, especially on categories where Dollar wants to expand. Store brands do not only need to stand in as ‘the cheapest option’ but also ensure a continued alignment to name brands (quality, pricing, and presence).
Evolve assortments but stay true to Dollar. New item and seasonal expansion has been noticed, but the channel still needs to feel like a quick, value-first trip.
