Executive Brief - Consumer Tech Trends 2027: Global Routes to Growth in the Era of "Justified Consumption"
Executive Summary
NIQ's Consumer Tech Trends 2027 report identifies a fundamental shift in consumer behavior: the world has entered an era of**"justified consumption."** Consumers continue to spend on technology and durable goods, but purchases are increasingly scrutinized through the lens of long-term value, utility, durability, confidence, and relevance. The purchase decision is shifting from*"Can I afford this?"* to*"Will I still believe this was worth it six months from now?"*
This shift is reshaping how manufacturers, retailers, and service providers must approach innovation, pricing, merchandising, product experiences, digital commerce, and customer engagement.
Key Learning #1
Growth Is Being Driven by Value, Not Volume
Global Consumer Tech & Durables (T&D) sales reached $857 billion in 2025 and are forecast to reach $1.4 trillion in 2026, with growth increasingly driven by pricing, premiumization, and consumer willingness to pay for products that deliver meaningful benefits. While global sales value grew 9% in Q1 2026, unit volumes declined by 1.2%, highlighting a market where consumers are buying more selectively but spending more when they perceive value.
Key Insight: Consumers are not abandoning categories. They are becoming more intentional about where they spend.
Implication: Winning brands must clearly communicate how products improve everyday life, save time, reduce effort, or improve long-term ownership value. Generic innovation alone is no longer enough.
Key Learning #2
Premiumization Is Thriving When Benefits Are Tangible
Across virtually every category, premium segments are significantly outperforming the overall category when the benefit is easily understood.
Examples include:
Robot vacuum cleaners: +37% growth
Wet & dry vacuum cleaners: +33% growth
Smartphones with ≥256GB storage: +29% growth
High-refresh-rate monitors: +23% growth
Large-screen (75"+) TVs: +13% growth versus 4% for TVs overall
Key Insight: Consumers willingly pay more when they can clearly see the practical benefit.
Implication: Future growth will come from simplifying the value proposition and making product benefits easier to understand, compare, and experience. Brands must communicate outcomes rather than specifications.
Key Learning #3
Regional Growth Dynamics Are Diverging
Growth opportunities vary significantly by geography.
Key Insight: Emerging markets continue to drive adoption and growth, while developed markets increasingly operate as premium profit pools.
Implication: Manufacturers and retailers need differentiated regional strategies. Success will require balancing high-growth emerging markets with premium-focused mature markets.
Key Learning #4
Consumers Are Price Sensitive, But Not Price Driven
The report highlights that:
63% of T&D consumers are highly price sensitive.
60% prioritize brands offering good value for money.
Durability, quality, convenience, and trust rank ahead of "inexpensive" when consumers define value.
Key Insight: Consumers are seeking the*"right price"* rather than the lowest price.
Implication: Pricing strategies should focus on confidence and value creation rather than simply discounting. Products that reduce risk and increase trust can sustain premium pricing.
Key Learning #5
Promotions Are Becoming Strategic Growth Engines
Promotional activity continues to influence purchasing decisions globally.
Key findings include:
56% delayed purchases because products were too expensive or they were waiting for discounts.
73% say promotions strongly influence purchase decisions.
The top seven annual promotion events account for 35% of annual T&D sales value.
Key Insight: Consumers increasingly use promotions to trade up rather than simply spend less.
Implication: The most effective promotional programs drive premium conversion, not just volume. Promotion strategy should be viewed as a core growth lever rather than a tactical pricing tool.
Key Learning #6
AI Is Valuable Only When It Solves Real Problems
AI awareness is becoming widespread:
57% understand what AI-powered features are.
Only 27% believe AI provides clear, meaningful benefits.
30% perceive AI primarily as marketing hype or struggle to see tangible value.
Key Insight: Consumers do not buy AI; they buy better outcomes enabled by AI.
Implication: Successful brands will translate AI into visible customer benefits such as convenience, personalization, efficiency, performance, energy savings, and reduced effort.
Key Learning #7
Ecosystems Are Becoming More Important Than Individual Devices
Modern consumers increasingly purchase products as part of connected ecosystems.
Key findings:
72% already have up to five connected devices in their homes.
78% say compatibility with existing products is important.
42% prefer open ecosystems that allow multiple brands versus only 20% who prefer a single-brand environment.
Key Insight: Consumers are evaluating experiences, not just products.
Implication: Product interoperability, ecosystem partnerships, and cross-category user experiences are becoming competitive differentiators.
Key Learning #8
Trust Is Becoming a Core Purchase Driver
Consumers increasingly seek reassurance before making technology purchases.
Influential decision factors include:
Clear product information: 75%
Promotions and discounts: 73%
Warranty and return policies: 71%
Brand reputation: 70%
Reviews and ratings: 65%
Key Insight: Trust has become as important as product performance.
Implication: Brands must invest in education, demonstrations, reviews, warranties, retail experiences, and proof points that reduce consumer uncertainty.
Key Learning #9
Circularity and Sustainability Have Become Economic Discussions
Consumers increasingly value sustainability, but through a practical lens.
Consumers rank the following as important purchase factors:
Durability: 86%
Repairability: 82%
Energy efficiency: 81%
Refurbishment is also gaining traction:
55% are familiar with refurbished options.
Approximately half would consider purchasing a refurbished product if supported by warranties and trusted certification.
Key Insight: Sustainability is increasingly viewed through cost savings and ownership value rather than environmental benefits alone.
Implication: Brands should position sustainability around lower operating costs, longer product life, repairability, and trade-in opportunities.
Key Learning #10
AI-Driven Discovery Will Redefine Commerce
Consumer discovery and evaluation are becoming increasingly digital.
65% start their product research online.
Online channels now represent 36% of global T&D sales.
Online penetration reaches 50%+ in IT and Small Domestic Appliances.
Key Insight: As AI increasingly mediates product discovery, digital visibility may become as important as physical shelf presence.
Implication: Winning brands will invest in machine-readable product content, digital shelf excellence, reviews, availability data, and omnichannel experiences that improve discoverability and confidence.
Executive Takeaway
The era of**"justified consumption"** represents a structural shift in how consumers evaluate technology purchases globally. The winners will not necessarily be the brands with the most innovation, the lowest prices, or the broadest distribution. Instead, they will be the brands that make value easiest to understand, trust, compare, and experience.
The report suggests five overarching imperatives for manufacturers and retailers:
Shift from innovation-led growth to justification-led growth.
Compete on total value, not price alone.
Design for both premiumization and value-seeking consumers.
Treat promotions as strategic demand architecture.
Optimize products and content for both human and AI-driven discovery.
At its core, the report argues that future growth belongs to organizations that help consumers confidently answer one simple question:
"Why should I buy this, and will I still be happy that I did?"
